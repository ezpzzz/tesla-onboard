## New pre-multi-tenant counsel items

1. **Indefinite retention of full guest mail.** The Workspace Mail design revises the prior 30-day-default retention posture to indefinite-by-default (Premise 3, D8: "indefinite retention of captured mail **plus first-class deletion machinery**... Config-driven retention job (default: keep forever; window adjustable without code)... the 30-day default is retired."). The prior counsel memo already flagged this exact axis as open and unresolved:
   > "Whether EVhost's own retention window for parsed guest data is defined and short (reduces exposure) vs. indefinite." (`docs/legal/2026-08-16-turo-tos-review.md:78`, "What would change the answer")

   Indefinite default retention of full guest email content (names, contact details, trip details, and any other PII Turo transmits in notification/message emails) increases exposure on this exact axis the prior memo left open. Counsel must confirm this is acceptable — or specify a mandated maximum window — before any workspace beyond the founder's own is onboarded. The design's config-driven, per-workspace retention window (5A: a nullable column on `onlyevs_email_integrations`, "null = forever") means any counsel-mandated window becomes a configuration change, not a migration, once decided — see item 4 below.

2. **Full-content inbox surface exceeds the review-only framing counsel evaluated.** The prior sign-off (`docs/legal/2026-08-16-turo-tos-review.md`, gate 4) was scoped to "own-workspace, Review-only intake scope" — structured candidate cards with, at most, a bounded plain-text excerpt (`facts.messageText`, capped at `MESSAGE_TEXT_MAX_CHARS` per the design's Constraints section). Workspace Mail adds a persistent, full-fidelity, rendered-HTML inbox (`/owner/mail`) that is a durable system of record for complete guest email content — a materially different data-exposure surface than what the prior review considered. This is a new fact pattern, not a restatement of the reviewed one, and needs its own counsel look before multi-tenant enablement: does presenting full original email content (vs. a bounded excerpt) change the controller/processor analysis, the minimization posture, or the notice obligations already flagged as open in the prior memo (`docs/legal/2026-08-16-turo-tos-review.md:72-74`, GDPR/CCPA controller-role discussion)?

3. **Plaintext body search index.** The design adds, for the first time, a searchable plaintext index of email body content: "Postgres holds metadata **plus, new, a private-schema extracted-plaintext search index**" (Constraints section), materialized as `private.onlyevs_email_message_index` (body_tsv column) queried via a hardened, membership-checked RPC, "never RLS-exposed directly" (Recommended Approach, step 1). This is new plaintext-at-rest in Postgres beyond the one plaintext copy the design doc itself flags as pre-existing (`onlyevs_email_candidates.proposed_state`, up to 20K chars of `messageText`/`guestMessageText`). A searchable full-text index of guest email content is a distinct, separately-reviewable exposure from the encrypted R2 envelope Workspace Mail otherwise treats as the source of truth — counsel should confirm this additional plaintext-at-rest surface, and the purge obligations attaching to it, before multi-tenant enablement. The design's own purge scope commits to scrubbing this index (Premise 3: "Purge scope enumerates EVERY body copy: R2 envelope, extracted attachment objects, private index rows (tsvector + snippet)..."), which is the engineering answer; counsel confirms whether that answer is sufficient.

4. **Retention-delta note: the prior memo endorsed 30-day auto-expiry; D8's purge machinery is the replacement answer to the same concern.** The email-ingest rollout doc's original infrastructure step 2 specified a blanket 30-day R2 lifecycle TTL as the retention control (`docs/rollouts/2026-08-16-email-ingest-rollout.md` §3.1, step 2, prior to the 2026-08-17 amendment). The Workspace Mail design's Dependencies section records this directly:
   > "the ladder's bucket-creation step previously specified a blanket 30-day R2 lifecycle TTL — incompatible with the D3/D8 indefinite-retention decision (raw mail would silently expire under a UI claiming a durable archive). The bucket must be created WITHOUT the blanket TTL; deletion is exclusively app-owned (purge machinery + config-driven retention sweep, which delete R2 objects themselves). New hard pre-enablement gate: verify the R2 lifecycle configuration matches the workspace retention config BEFORE intake goes live... Counsel checklist notes the delta (the memo endorsed auto-expiry; D8's purge machinery is the replacement answer to the same concern)."

   Counsel evaluated (implicitly, by not objecting to it as drafted) a world where storage itself expired guest mail after 30 days as a bounded-exposure control. That control no longer exists as a storage-layer guarantee — it is replaced by application-layer purge machinery (message/reservation/guest-scoped deletion functions) and a config-driven retention sweep, both of which are only as reliable as the code that runs them, not a provider-enforced bucket rule. Counsel should be told this delta explicitly and confirm whether app-owned deletion machinery is an acceptable substitute for the storage-layer TTL it evaluated, or whether a specific enforced-at-the-storage-layer window is still required. Cross-reference: `docs/rollouts/2026-08-16-email-ingest-rollout.md` §3.1's 2026-08-17 amendment note on step 2.
